The Things People Don't Say

M. Dimas K. G.
July 27, 2026 • 3 Min Read

I used to think the hardest part of creative work was coming up with ideas.

Turns out, ideas are everywhere. They're in every moodboard, every trending audio, every "save this for later" post. The hard part is knowing which idea is actually worth making.

Lately I've been paying more attention to people than to references.

Not in a creepy way. Just... watching.

How someone taps their foot while waiting. Why a student keeps using the same backpack even though the zipper barely works. Why people instinctively replay a video but never press like.

Those moments don't look like insights at first. They just look ordinary.

Maybe that's the point.

I'm starting to think creativity isn't about inventing something nobody has seen before. It's about noticing something everyone has seen, but nobody thought was worth talking about.

That's probably why I care more about the small execution details than chasing a perfect aesthetic.

A visual can be beautiful and still leave nothing behind.

But sometimes a small observation sticks with people longer than the cleanest edit or the nicest color grade.

I'm still figuring this out.

Maybe that's what keeps creative work interesting. Every project is another excuse to pay closer attention.◼
