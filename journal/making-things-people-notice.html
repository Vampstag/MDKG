On Making Things People Actually Notice

M. Dimas K. G.
July 27, 2026 • 3 Min Read

Lately I've been thinking that good creative work doesn't start with ideas. It starts with paying attention.

Not just to trends or references, but to the little things people do without realizing it. The pause before answering. The way someone holds onto an old school bag long after it's worn out. The joke everyone understands without needing context. Those moments usually look too ordinary to matter, until they become the reason a piece of work feels familiar.

I used to think creativity meant adding something new. Now I think it's often about noticing something that was already there.

That's changed how I approach almost everything. Before opening Premiere, Figma, or a camera, I spend more time asking why someone would care in the first place. Sometimes the best creative decision happens long before making anything.

The tools still matter. So does craft. But I've found that people rarely remember a visual because it was technically perfect. They remember it because it reminded them of something they already felt but hadn't put into words.

Maybe that's what I'm trying to make.

Not just visuals people can see, but visuals that make people quietly think, "Yeah... I know that feeling."◼
