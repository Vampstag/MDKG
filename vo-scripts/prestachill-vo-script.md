# VO Script — Torch Prestachill Case Study
Target length: ~90–100 seconds. Tone: direct, first person, matter-of-fact — no hype-reel delivery. Matches the page's "Listen to Project" narrator slot at `#overview` (audio file: `assets/audio/vo-prestachill.mp3`).

---

**[Overview]**

Torch's Back to School 2026 campaign was built on a simple idea: not every win has to be big. Getting to school on time. Remembering to pray between Mobile Legends matches. Making it through the week without blowing your allowance. Those count too. That's PrestaChill.

I worked from Torch's creative direction and handled the execution — thirty-eight videos over two and a half months, across organic social, e-commerce, and paid ads. I shot them, cut them, and graded them.

**[The Challenge]**

Here's the problem going in: Torch's existing audience was the wrong audience. Most of the people following the brand were parents buying school bags. This campaign needed to reach their kids — who barely knew Torch existed. Dads pay, but kids pick.

**[The Approach]**

So instead of borrowing trust from the brand, I borrowed it from the feed. I tracked what was trending on TikTok that week, brought the formats and sounds worth adapting to the team, and shot for whichever one got the green light. The content earned attention first. The brand came second.

Behind that, every shoot had different light and an uncontrolled location, so I corrected footage using scopes in Resolve and built a consistent grade to hold the campaign together visually.

**[Selected Outputs]**

Across the thirty-eight videos, a few stood out. "Bag Theory" — two minutes on how Indonesian school bags changed since the seventies — pulled the highest engagement in the whole campaign. A street interview series asked students what actually counts as an achievement. Turns out it's not just trophies — it's saying good morning to your parents, or just getting through the week without going broke.

**[The Impact]**

No paid spend behind any of it, so the numbers that matter are engagement, not just views. Bag Theory hit a 4.51% engagement rate on Instagram — about three-point-six times the campaign's other posts. Across TikTok and Instagram combined, the campaign pulled four hundred fifty-three thousand, five hundred and ten organic views.

**[Key Insights]**

Three things stuck with me. Length didn't hold engagement back — Bag Theory ran long for short-form and still won. Nostalgia gave an unfamiliar brand somewhere to land, since that video wasn't really about Torch at all. And no single editing formula would have worked across the campaign — a challenge video and a packing video need opposite rhythms. What held it together was the idea, not the cutting style.

That's PrestaChill.
