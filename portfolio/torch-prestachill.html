Torch's Back to School 2026 campaign was built on a simple idea: not every win has to be big. Getting to school on time. Remembering to pray between Mobile Legends matches. Making it through the week without blowing your allowance. Those count too. That's PrestaChill.

I worked from Torch's creative direction and handled the execution. 38 videos over two and a half months, across organic social, e-commerce, and paid ads. I shot them, cut them, and graded them.

Duration 2,5 Months
Industry Fashion / Retail
"Creating short-form content for PrestaChill that reminds students to celebrate their small wins."

How I'd describe the assignment

The Challenge

Torch's existing audience was the wrong audience.

Most of the people following Torch were parents buying school bags. This campaign needed to reach their kids, who barely knew the brand existed. Dads pay, but kids pick.

The Approach

[01]

Borrowing trust from the feed, not the brand.

Torch had no recognition with this audience, so the content borrowed it from the platform instead. I tracked what was trending on TikTok that week, brought the formats, sounds, and pacing worth adapting to the team, and shot for whichever one got the green light. The content earned attention first. The brand came second.

0:00/0:00
[02]

Matching shots, not the weather.

Changing light and uncontrolled locations meant every shoot looked different. I corrected footage using scopes in Resolve, built a PowerGrade as a consistent starting point, and kept the final grade aligned with the campaign's creative direction.

Key Insights

Length didn't hold engagement back.

Bag Theory ran two minutes, long for short-form, and still landed the campaign's highest engagement rate at 4.51%, roughly 2x the next best video. The short-form instinct is to cut for length. This suggests the story earning its runtime matters more than the runtime itself.

Nostalgia gave an unfamiliar brand somewhere to land.

Bag Theory wasn't about Torch. It was about school bag history most of the audience never lived through. It's one video, not proof, but the engagement suggests that gave people a reason to stay before the brand introduced itself.

One editing formula would have flattened the campaign.

A challenge video and a packing video need opposite rhythms. What held the work together was the idea, not the cutting style.
