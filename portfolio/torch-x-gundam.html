Torch's first collaboration with Mobile Suit Gundam, developed to bring the iconic mecha franchise into Torch's visual language while connecting with its existing fan community.

Working within Torch's creative direction, I handled the visual execution across the campaign's key video deliverables, from teaser films and the main launch film to social content and supporting campaign assets.

My role covered cinematography, editing, and color grading, with a focus on building a cinematic visual language that felt industrial, atmospheric, and consistent across different production setups and formats.

The Challenge
[01]
Maintaining a Consistent Visual Language

The campaign needed to feel cohesive across multiple shoots, formats, and deliverables, despite differences in lighting, setups, and production conditions.

[02]
Working With Production Constraints

Limited setups, changing conditions, and tight production timelines meant the visual approach had to remain flexible throughout the shoot rather than relying on a fixed setup.

[03]
Making the Collaboration Feel Authentic

Gundam already carries a strong visual identity and an established fan culture. The challenge was to reference that world without letting the IP overpower Torch's own design language.

Creative Approach

The visual treatment was developed around muted industrial tones, restrained contrast, and subtle retro-sci-fi texture. The grade was built to give the campaign a distinct atmosphere while remaining flexible enough to work across footage captured in different setups.

The production environment varied throughout the shoot, with changing lighting conditions, tight schedules, and limited opportunities for reshoots.

Rather than forcing every shot into an identical treatment, the grade was adapted to preserve the character of each setup while bringing the footage into a cohesive visual language.

Key Insights

Process Can Be Part of the Story

The strongest-performing piece was not the main announcement, but Behind the Design, which reached 317K views. The result suggested that the audience had an interest in seeing how the collaboration came together, not only in seeing the final product.

Authenticity Comes From the Details

The Gundam identity did not need to be reproduced literally. Color, pacing, atmosphere, and visual references were enough to create a connection to the franchise while keeping the work within Torch's visual world.

Adaptation Is Part of the Craft

Different shooting conditions naturally produced different-looking footage. The goal was not to make every setup identical, but to use production and post-production decisions to make the final campaign feel like one cohesive body of work.
